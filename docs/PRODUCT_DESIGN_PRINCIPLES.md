# Big Exec Product Design Principles

These are acceptance requirements, not optional polish.

1. **UX/UI is P0.** Functional engineering and product design advance together. A manager-facing or commissioner-facing flow is not beta-ready until its information hierarchy, responsive behavior, loading/empty/error states, accessibility basics, and Big Exec visual identity are complete and reviewed.
2. **One connected product.** Every authenticated page uses the same Big Exec product shell, navigation language, hierarchy, component language, spacing rhythm, responsive rules, and black/gold brand foundation. Page-specific atmosphere may vary without making the page feel like a different application.
3. **Left-side product navigation.** The persistent authenticated product destinations are Front Office, Matchup, Locker Room, League, and Stadium. Desktop uses a persistent left rail. Mobile preserves the same information architecture through a left-side navigation drawer/sheet. Primary navigation order must not change between pages.
4. **Front Office is the command center.** The Front Office header prioritizes franchise/team name, manager name, and record at top-left and league name at top-right. Its four primary cards are Draft Room/Free Agency, Locker Room, Trade Room, and League News. The Draft Room card automatically becomes Free Agency once the league draft is complete.
5. **Beginner-first, expert-capable.** A first-time fantasy player must understand the next action without knowing players, teams, roster terminology, or strategy. Experienced players must be able to reach deeper statistics and faster controls without slowing the beginner path.
6. **Guide roster construction.** Draft and lineup experiences explain required positions, support position-first browsing, allow direct search, expose availability, prevent accidental actions, and never require scrolling a complete player database to perform a basic task.
7. **Best product, transparent cost.** Zero current users is a cost-planning fact, not a reduced-quality target. Proposals should identify the strongest experience, implementation options, recurring costs, and scaling thresholds.
8. **Original visual identity.** Use Big Exec marks and generated franchise crests only. Do not use professional-league branding, professional-team logos, copied uniforms, player photographs, recognizable player likenesses, or trophies/awards that imitate recognizable NFL awards such as the Lombardi Trophy. Player names, factual statistics, game status, and results are informational data subject to provider terms and legal review.
9. **Create Big Exec's own championship identity.** The Big Exec Champions Trophy must be an original architectural design, not a football-on-pedestal or professional-league trophy lookalike. It belongs in the winning franchise's Owner's Office and persists as part of franchise legacy.
10. **Franchise ownership matters.** Franchise name, abbreviation, colors, crest, stadium, Owner's Office, achievements, rivalries, championship history, and legacy must feel persistent and connected throughout the product.
11. **League information should feel alive.** League News, Locker Room, standings, trades, weekly performances, rivalries, playoff movement, awards, recaps, and legacy events should present deterministic league facts with sports-broadcast/newsroom energy instead of database-table presentation.
12. **Accessible by default.** Keyboard access, visible focus, semantic labels, screen-reader status, reduced motion, readable contrast, and mobile touch targets are release requirements.
13. **Prove critical promises.** Realtime drafting, timers, autopick, scoring, waivers, trading, season automation, stadium progression, and recap rendering remain unproven until verified end to end in production-like conditions. No gate passes on screenshots or builds alone.
