## Lineups

### BB-072 — What is a daily lineup league?

**Answer:** A daily league lets you change starters each day before those players lock.

### BB-073 — What is a weekly lineup league?

**Answer:** A weekly league makes you set your team for a full week. You do it before the league's lock time.

### BB-074 — When does a basketball player lock?

**Answer:** In many daily leagues, a player locks when that player's real game starts. Your league rule may differ.

### BB-079 — What if all my lineup spots are full today?

**Answer:** You may have to choose which players to start. Check which stats or points you need most.

### BB-080 — What if a good player is on my bench but plays today?

**Answer:** Their stats may not count if they stay on the bench when the game starts.

### BB-081 — Should I fill every open starting spot?

**Answer:** Most of the time, yes. But a games cap or team plan may make an open spot less useful.

### BB-082 — What is a busy game day?

**Answer:** A busy day is when many real teams play. You may have more players than starting spots.

### BB-083 — What is a light game day?

**Answer:** A light day is when fewer real teams play. It can be easier to fit all your players into the lineup.

## Schedule

### BB-084 — Why do games played matter in fantasy basketball?

**Answer:** More real games can give your players more chances to add points or stats.

### BB-085 — Why does my opponent have more games this week?

**Answer:** Their players may have more real games on the schedule. That can give them more chances to add stats.

### BB-086 — What is streaming?

**Answer:** Streaming means adding a player for a short time to get more games or key stats.

### BB-087 — What is a streamer?

**Answer:** A streamer is a player you add for a short time. They may help on a key day or with one stat.

### BB-088 — How do I stream players?

**Answer:** Look for an open player who plays when you have room. Make sure the add fits your weekly move limit.

### BB-089 — What is a back-to-back?

**Answer:** A back-to-back means a real team plays on two days in a row.

### BB-090 — Why are back-to-backs useful in fantasy?

**Answer:** They can give one player two game chances in a short time. They can also bring more rest risk for some players.

### BB-091 — What is a low-game day?

**Answer:** A low-game day is a day when fewer real teams play. A streamer may fit your lineup more easily then.

### BB-092 — What is a high-game day?

**Answer:** A high-game day is a day when many real teams play. Some of your players may be stuck on the bench.

### BB-093 — What is schedule volume?

**Answer:** Schedule volume is about games. It means how many real games your players have in a set time.

### BB-094 — Should I add a worse player who has more games this week?

**Answer:** Sometimes. More games can help, but player skill and your scoring needs still matter. I can compare both choices.

**Answer type:** Dynamic — check live state before answering.

**Internal tool note:** schedule + scoring + player outlook

### BB-095 — What is a games cap?

**Answer:** A games cap is a set limit on games that can count. It may apply to a spot or the full season.

### BB-096 — What happens if I reach a games cap?

**Answer:** New games at that capped spot may stop counting. The exact rule depends on your league.

### BB-097 — Do all basketball leagues have a games cap?

**Answer:** No. Some do and some do not. I should check your league setting.

### BB-098 — What is an add limit?

**Answer:** An add limit is the most players you can pick up in a set time, such as one week.

### BB-099 — Why does an add limit matter for streaming?

**Answer:** Each stream may use one add. If you use them too fast, you may have none left when you need one.

### BB-100 — How far ahead should I look at the schedule?

**Answer:** A few days and the full week can both help. Look for injuries, open lineup spots, and teams with useful game days.
